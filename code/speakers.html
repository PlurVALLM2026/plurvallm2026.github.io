Invited speakers

Rostam J. Neuwirth

University of Macau

Rostam J. Neuwirth (諾思坦) is Distinguished Professor of Law and Head for Department of Global Legal Studies at the University of Macau. Previously, he taught at the West Bengal University of Juridical Sciences (NUJS) in Kolkata and the Hidayatullah National Law University (HNLU) in Raipur (India) and worked as a legal adviser in the Department of European Law of the International Law Bureau of the Austrian Federal Ministry for Foreign Affairs. He received his PhD degree from the European University Institute (EUI) in Florence (Italy) and also holds a master’s degree in law (LLM) from the Faculty of Law of McGill University in Montreal (Canada). As an undergraduate he studied at the University of Graz (Austria) and the Université d’Auvergne (France). He is the author of The EU Artificial Intelligence Act: Regulating Subliminal AI Systems (Routledge 2023), which was also translated into Chinese, as well as Law in the Time of Oxymora: A Synaesthesia of Language, Logic and Law (Routledge 2018). He has also published extensively in international journals on contemporary global legal issues, exploring the intrinsic linkages between law, on the one hand, and language, cognition, art, culture, society, and technology, on the other.

Nancy F. Chen

Center for Frontier AI Research, A*STAR

Talk: Grounding AI in Society

I study communication across human–human, human–machine, and human–AI contexts, with particular attention to how expectations of communication shift across them. We expect humans to understand context, intent, emotion, and social norms; machines to be predictable, efficient, and reliable; and AI systems increasingly to satisfy both sets of expectations. This convergence creates new questions about trust, agency, accountability, and what constitutes meaningful communication and collaboration between humans and AI. Against this backdrop, my work develops computational models of human communication and context, builds socially grounded technologies for trustworthy human–AI interaction, and investigates how language-model use reshapes learning, reasoning, and professional expertise. The unifying goal is to augment human capability and potential while preserving human agency and cognitive resilience. I illustrate this research agenda through case studies spanning healthcare, education, eldercare, collaborative learning, and digital services.

Nancy F. Chen is a Lead Principal Investigator and leads the Ethical and Trustworthy AI pillar at Center for Frontier AI Research, A*STAR. Her work focuses on building human-centered frontier AI that can reason, communicate, and act responsibly in diverse social, cultural, and linguistic contexts. She works across trustworthy and agentic AI, multimodal and multilingual foundation models, speech and conversational intelligence, and human–AI collaboration. Her research has increasingly explored how AI systems understand not only language and perception, but also people, values, emotions, culture, and social context. She has led work on culturally grounded AI, multimodal reasoning, AI safety and evaluation, educational AI, and socially intelligent interactive systems, with applications spanning public-sector and real-world deployments in Singapore. Chen received her PhD at MIT and Harvard. She is an ISCA Fellow and AAIA Fellow and has held leadership roles at major AI conferences, including serving as Program Chair of NeurIPS 2025 and ICLR 2023.

Roy Lee

Singapore University of Technology and Design (SUTD)

Roy Lee is Associate Professor and Associate Head of Pillar (Research) in Information Systems Technology and Design (ISTD) at the Singapore University of Technology and Design. His work sits at the intersection of responsible AI, social computing, NLP, online safety, hate speech, and low-resource LLM safety. His research highlights cyber social safety and NLP/social computing, and his recent speaking record includes keynotes on multimodal AI for low-resource/social-impact contexts and AI for safer online spaces. His group also has recent work on safety alignment in low-resource English languages, including a best-paper recognition at the AAAI 2026 LM4UC workshop. Profile: https://www.sutd.edu.sg/profile/roy-lee/

Pak-Hang Wong

Hong Kong Baptist University

Pak-Hang Wong is a philosopher and ethicist of technology based in the Academy of Chinese, History, Religion and Philosophy and a Research Fellow at the Centre for Applied Ethics at Hong Kong Baptist University. His research explores the social, ethical, and political dimensions of AI and emerging digital technologies, particularly from an intercultural perspective. He is Editor-in-Chief (Ethics, AI & Data) of Ethics and Society and Associate Editor (Epistemology and Technology) of Philosophy & Technology. He co-edited Harmonious Technology: A Confucian Ethics of Technology, which offers an alternative, non-Western perspective on the philosophy and ethics of technology.
